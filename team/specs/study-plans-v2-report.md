Run 11's built-ins divided chapter counts evenly (Gospels 89 chapters /

demand Psalm 119's 176 verses next to a day of three short chapters.

## Adopted daily target, with its research basis

**40 verses per day**, constant `wwBuiltinVersesPerDay`.

Research (recorded in the producer brief, `team/specs/study-plans-v2-brief.md`):
one-year whole-Bible plans ask ~86 verses/day (3¼ chapters) — the load
being rejected; two-year plans ask ~43 verses/day at 4–6 minutes/day
(My Bible Log reading-time guide; Thomas Nelson two-year plan);
bible270 sizes days by verse count rather than chapter count. The studio
adopted 40 verses/day, on the order of one ordinary chapter a day.

## New built-in durations (consequences of the target, real data)

Computed on the bundled `data.json` (31,102 verses, 1,189 chapters):

| Plan | Chapters | Days |
|---|---|---|
| The Gospels | 89 | **87 days** |
| Psalms & Proverbs | 181 | **84 days** |
| The Whole Bible | 1,189 | **767 days** |

Engine (`lib/data/plans.dart`), one model for built-ins and customs:
Mode A packs chapters toward a verses/day target (closest-fit greedy);
Mode B partitions into exactly N days by cumulative verse thresholds.
Chapters are atomic and never split; no day is empty. Ratified heaviest
days (Petra's independent recomputation over the full corpus, which
corrected the brief's draft figure): Numbers 7 alone = 89 verses
(Whole Bible day 96), 1 Chronicles 6 alone = 81, Luke 1 alone = 80,
heaviest multi-chapter day = 71. The old rule's peak days were
187/191/212 verses.

## Migration rule

Progress lives on chapters, not days: the stored `startedOn` + `done`
set of global chapter indices is untouched by schedule re-derivation.
Schedules are derived, never stored, so when the built-ins were
rebalanced every existing check mark and the percent survived
identically; only `currentDay` recomputes against the new day count.
No migration code, no silent loss.

## Percent complete (founder fold-in)

Per-chapter check-off stays the core interaction on every plan. An
explicit `<pct>%` number renders beside the progress bar on the Home
card AND the plan detail screen, both from the single `planPercent`
helper, so the surfaces cannot disagree. Widget tests assert the
number updates on check AND on uncheck on both surfaces.

## Custom plans, as built

- Storage: plans document v2 under the SAME prefs key `ww_plans_v1`
  (`{version:2, progress:{…v1 payload…}, custom:{profileId:[defs]},
  customSeq:{profileId:n}}`); ids `custom-<n>` are never reused.
- Coverage: segments over core BibleData books — whole books or a
  chapter range per segment.
- Pace: verses/day (Mode A) or finish-in-days (Mode B), generated by
  the same engine as the built-ins.
- Surfaces: the same Home card + progress bar, the same detail screen
  (generalized via `ResolvedPlan`) with per-chapter toggles and
  Start/Restart semantics, plus Edit and Delete (confirm-guarded) for
  customs only. Home shows built-ins, then customs, then one
  "Create your own plan" tile; the section renders even with zero
  offered plans.
- Dormancy (run 10/11 rule): a custom plan is offered only while every
  segment book is in the active canon; otherwise it hides with its
  bytes untouched and returns intact on switching back.

## Backups (producer decision, recorded in the brief)

Plans JOIN the backup: the exported document gains a top-level `plans`
member carrying custom definitions + all plan progress. A strict
validator runs in front (`BackupImportError.invalidPlans`; dialog
sentence: "This backup's study plans are not in a form Woven Word can
import."); a malformed member rejects the WHOLE import and writes
nothing. Backups without the member (pre-package) import as before and
leave plans state untouched. `UserData.version` stays 3.

Gate finding F1 (P2) and its fix: the first build could show the import
success snackbar even if the plans write failed. Fixed in `68dbe4a` —
import replaces plans through a freshly loaded PlansStore with an
awaited save, and a `catch { return; }` makes the snackbar unreachable
on a failed write. Petra verified the fix by code read at re-check
(her gate's stated method for this item). Producer note: no
failure-injection test covers that path; accepted as a follow-up
candidate, not a landing blocker — the mechanism is a two-line control
flow verified by read, and the surrounding backup suites assert
byte-identity on every rejection and Cancel path.
